Mammals

Warm-blooded, mostly furry, and famously good at raising their young.

No. 001

African Lion

Panthera leo

The only cat that lives in social groups called prides. A male's roar can be heard up to 8 km away.

No. 002

African Elephant

Loxodonta africana

The largest land animal alive today. Elephants can recognize themselves in a mirror — a rare sign of self-awareness.

No. 003

Grey Wolf

Canis lupus

Wolves live in tight-knit packs led by a breeding pair, and can travel over 30 km in a single day hunting.

Birds

Feathered, mostly airborne, and the closest living relatives of dinosaurs.

No. 004

Bald Eagle

Haliaeetus leucocephalus

The national bird of the USA has eyesight roughly four to five times sharper than a human's.

No. 005

Emperor Penguin

Aptenodytes forsteri

The tallest penguin species. Fathers keep eggs warm on their feet for around 65 days through Antarctic winter.

No. 006

Indian Peafowl

Pavo cristatus

Only males — peacocks — grow the iconic tail. It's fanned open mainly to impress potential mates.

Reptiles

Scaly, cold-blooded, and often older evolutionary lineages than you'd expect.

No. 007

Komodo Dragon

Varanus komodoensis

The largest living lizard, found on just a few Indonesian islands, capable of taking down prey far bigger than itself.

No. 008

Sea Turtle

Chelonia mydas

Females return to the very beach they hatched on, sometimes decades later, to lay their own eggs.

No. 009

Chameleon

Chamaeleonidae

Their eyes can rotate independently of each other, giving them a nearly 360° view without moving their head.

Ocean Life

Over 70% of Earth is ocean, and most of it is still unmapped territory for these species.

No. 010

Blue Whale

Balaenoptera musculus

The largest animal to have ever existed on Earth — even bigger than the largest known dinosaurs.

No. 011

Clownfish

Amphiprioninae

Immune to the sting of the sea anemones they live in, giving them a safe home few other fish can enter.

No. 012

Octopus

Octopoda

Has three hearts and blue blood, and can squeeze its entire boneless body through openings the size of a coin.

Insects

Small in size, enormous in impact — insects pollinate most of the plants we eat.

No. 013

Monarch Butterfly

Danaus plexippus

Migrates up to 4,800 km between North America and Mexico — a journey no single butterfly completes alone.

No. 014

Honeybee

Apis mellifera

Communicates the location of food to the rest of the hive through a "waggle dance."

No. 015

Ladybug

Coccinellidae

A single ladybug can eat over 5,000 aphids in its lifetime, making it a farmer's best friend.

Endangered Species

These animals need our attention most. Play clips directly inside each specimen card.

No. 016

Giant Panda

Ailuropoda melanoleuca

Eats almost nothing but bamboo — up to 14 hours a day — despite having the digestive system of a carnivore.

No. 017

Sumatran Tiger

Panthera tigris sumatrae

Fewer than 700 are believed to remain in the wild, found only on the Indonesian island of Sumatra.

No. 018

Black Rhino

Diceros bicornis

Poaching for its horn has driven this species to the brink; conservation programs are slowly rebuilding numbers.
